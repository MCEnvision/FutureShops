## Purpose and Ownership

This phase reproduces, repairs, integrates, and verifies Forge 1.20.1 issues 25, 32, and 34 without deleting affected data, weakening readiness, or losing or duplicating items or currency. It owns the detailed execution of `CORE-REQ-004`, `CORE-REQ-005`, and `CORE-REQ-006` only. The frozen master owns product scope, requirement assignment, support-line topology, owner decisions, external prerequisites, and the final completion endpoint.

The phase has three independently traceable defect streams joined by one Forge integration gate:

1. Issue 25 traces catalog parsing, beta-version transition, durable stock activation, module readiness, authoritative offer publication, and buy, direct-sell, and bulk-sell visibility. It preserves the post-`404c011` failure as decisive evidence that the first migration repair did not close the report. Local transition and supported-current-state fixtures determine whether each failure is a current defect or an expected unsupported intermediate-beta incompatibility.
2. Issue 32 deliberately generates and minimizes corrupt, truncated, partial, newer-schema, incompatible, and ownership-conflicting FutureShops and player states. Bounded codec fuzzing, lifecycle-order fuzzing, crash cuts, modded-item sentinels, and unrelated-player-NBT sentinels isolate a FutureShops-owned cause from malformed base-game or other-mod state and prove non-destructive recovery.
3. Issue 34 compares finite- and infinite-stock money purchases at the first divergent authoritative boundary, then proves reservation, payment, delivery, claim, replay, persistence, and conservation behavior on an isolated dedicated Forge server with at least two independent clients.

`CORE-REQ-012`, `CORE-REQ-013`, and `CORE-REQ-014` are inherited persistence, integration, and economic contracts for these repairs. Their complete codebase-wide audits remain owned by later phases. This phase must not claim those later requirements complete.

## Architecture and Implementation Boundaries

### State Ownership and Dependency Direction

| State or transition | Authoritative owner | Readers and projections | Persistence boundary | Phase rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shop JSON and parsed definitions | `ShopDefinitionLoader` validates files; `ShopCatalog` owns the published in-memory snapshot | Server offer services, sell routing, client snapshots, admin diagnostics | `config/futureshops/shops/` with complete candidate validation and atomic writer paths | Invalid input cannot replace the last valid snapshot or make unrelated valid offers disappear without an exact failure classification. |

| Purchase request identity | Server request boundary and transaction service | Replay lookup, result packets, audit diagnostics | Stable request UUID in intent, journal, transaction, replay, stock, and claim records | A logical retry reuses its request identity. | Diagnostics | Authoritative boundary that detects the failure | Localized client response, bounded server log, operator status | Logs and issue evidence, not product truth | Include stable codes and correlation identities but exclude credentials, raw private player data, sensitive NBT, and exploit-enabling details. |

### Transaction Boundary

For issue 34 and any issue 32 path involving value movement, the required order is validate request and permissions, resolve authoritative listing and price, verify readiness, construct stable identity and fingerprint, reserve finite stock when applicable, prepare payment and item custody, durably record intent, commit authoritative state, attempt delivery, create or retain a durable claim on delivery failure, record terminal replay evidence, and publish a bounded result. An implementation may use existing internal substeps, but no repair may reverse these ownership rules or create a gap in which both parties own value or neither can recover it.

The finite-stock and infinite-stock paths must share request validation, authoritative pricing, payment, commit, delivery, claims, replay, and diagnostics. Their only intended difference is that finite stock creates and settles a durable bounded stock reservation while infinite stock does not decrement a finite quantity. The reproduction must find the earliest actual divergence rather than assuming the reservation itself is faulty.

### Persistence and Recovery Boundary

- Every evidence-bearing original is immutable. Copy the complete matching world, `playerdata`, FutureShops SavedData, journal, checkpoint, ledger, custody, claims, configuration, and relevant logs before a mutation test.
- Keep a manifest of source path class, schema or format, size, hash, acquisition time, redaction, related revision, and restore group. Never publish raw player UUIDs, private NBT, or unrelated mod data.
- If a changed schema is necessary, define current and legacy readers, target schema, migration trigger, idempotency, atomic replacement, last-known-good behavior, backup point, rollback boundary, and malformed-data response before implementation.
- Bound every codec and fuzz input by the supported storage and network limits before allocation or traversal. The corpus must include exact boundary values, one-over-bound rejection, truncation at every structural boundary, missing and duplicate members, wrong tag types, newer versions, incompatible legacy versions, conflicting ownership records, and valid unknown compatible fields. A minimized failing seed becomes a permanent regression fixture.
- Lifecycle fuzzing permutes only semantically reachable orders around login, inventory mutation, receipt creation, player save, file and directory force, transaction intent, commit, claim creation, logout, shutdown, restart, and reconnect. Impossible platform sequences are retained as invalid-test controls rather than misclassified as product defects.
- A failed test stops the isolated server, preserves the resulting state and logs, and restores the entire matching fixture group. Do not restore or delete one selected file from a multi-file recovery lineage.
- A player-data repair may modify only proven FutureShops-owned state. Platform-level NBT ordering differences are compared canonically, while field presence, values, item identity, capabilities, unrelated mod payloads, explicit unrelated-player-NBT sentinels, and modded-item sentinels must remain equivalent.

### Networking and Client Boundary

The server validates packet direction, authenticated player identity, route or session nonce, request UUID, payload bounds, listing and option identity, quantity, payment source, current catalog revision, readiness, permissions, and authoritative state. The client may display a cached catalog or error result but may not author price, stock, ownership, completion, migration state, or recovery outcome. Stale snapshots produce a stable retry or refresh result without duplicate mutation.

If a result code, payload, or protocol representation must change, preserve compatibility or version the protocol explicitly and test mismatched peers. A logging-only repair must not expose private NBT, balances unrelated to the request, filesystem paths outside approved operator context, or complete player state.

### Concurrency and Performance

- All authoritative mutation occurs on the logical server or through the existing server-thread and synchronized repository contracts.
- Concurrent finite-stock buyers are serialized or compare-and-set against an exact revision. At most available stock succeeds, and every rejection occurs before charge or receives deterministic compensation.
- Reconnect, retransmission, and restart use persisted request and transaction identity. They do not create new logical purchases.
- No new full catalog scan, filesystem operation, player-file force, broad inventory serialization, or unbounded diagnostic runs per tick, render, or packet hot path.
- Recovery work remains bounded per tick. Diagnostics aggregate repeated identical failures and retain one actionable record rather than spam.
